1. Official Academic Abstract (By Claude)
Conventional music instruction typically introduces staff notation before learners can internally hear, sing, and name the sounds it represents. Jinta-Keeta | Music Language: Neo Music Theory proposes reversing this order by treating music as a language acquired through the same sequence as speech: listening with comprehension, vocal production (humming), writing, and finally reading. The framework builds on established traditions of relative solmization and numbered-degree notation, organizing them into a distinctive pedagogical system with a Thai-language note chart.
Its central mechanism is Talented Relative Pitch (TRP), a staged training process in which learners internalize scale-degree numbers 1–7, rehearse them as pure hummed pitch, and then map them onto letter-named keys. The goal is bidirectional fluency: transcribing heard or imagined melodies into notation, and vocalizing notated music without an external instrument. Tonal organization is anchored on degree 1 for major and degree 6 for minor, treated as relative modes sharing a single key signature. A fixed 7×7 matrix of seven key rows by seven scale positions, together with a "Rule of Seven" for key signatures (paired sharp and flat counts summing to seven), is presented as a closed, ordinal-based system supporting transposition and mode change.
The framework is released under a Creative Commons CC BY-NC-ND license, with the stated aim of free access for learners worldwide and reduced inequality in music education, consistent with UNESCO Sustainable Development Goals 4 and 10.
